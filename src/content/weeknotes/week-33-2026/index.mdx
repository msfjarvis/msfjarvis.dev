### Sidebar: Codex Cloud is a terrible product

After struggling to even find where the option for it was, I finally kicked the tires on [Codex Cloud](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/cloud) to solve a longstanding gripe I have with WearOS version of Google Calendar: it refuses to sync non-Google calendars. This is not a problem shared by the Android counterpart, so I can only assume this is the result of incompetence.

With my gripe about Google Calendar and my unfulfilled cope of learning WearOS development as an excuse to get this expensive Pixel Watch, I decided I'd build my own calendar (with some slop involved). I yapped at the ChatGPT app to explain my problems with Google Calendar, what I wanted out of my ideal replacement and the general architecture I wanted to go for. The clanker suggested making the watch app a simple presenter for JSON encoded calendar events that a companion phone app could periodically send to it, which would significantly reduce complexity and make it much more battery efficient. I couldn't really find a fault in that since it still checks off all my boxes so that's what I went with.

Speaking of incompetence, that is also a quality shared by the people who run security at OpenAI and not just because of their recent accomplishments on the [FelonyBench](https://www.felonybench.com/) rankings. Codex Cloud does not let you export your changes from their sandbox to GitHub if the changes contain a binary file. OpenAI is aware that people do not want this nonsense [for nearly a year](https://github.com/openai/codex/issues/4867) and they've not changed anything here. Trying to set up an Android project by necessity means you have a small Gradle wrapper JAR in your source tree, which means you cannot bootstrap an Android app inside Codex Cloud. I deleted the JAR just so I could get the code out and vowed to never use this abomination ever again.
